# Find opportunities in a portfolio

Start with the finding most likely to matter to this investor. Explain in everyday language what could improve, by roughly how much, and what it would cost or put at risk. Keeping the current arrangement is a valid outcome.

## Start from the investor's position

Use supplied holdings or the authorised account's `get_account_summary` and unfiltered `list_positions`. Record the date, coverage and currency. Use each holding's `walletImpact.currentValue` and its currency. Keep demo accounts separate and check for the same account under multiple labels. Respect the user's targets, risk limits, intended holding period and money needed soon. If a missing goal would change the ranking, ask one focused question and give the observations the available figures support. Keep balances, quantities and account identifiers out of public searches.

## Test the alternatives

Compare each candidate with staying put or directing the next contribution differently. Show up to three findings with the clearest evidence and meaningful benefit. A missing country, sector or security can be an intentional choice. Use the investor's own target or a stated research question to explain why an absence matters.

- **Concentration or missing exposure.** Combine direct holdings with the same issuer inside funds using dated fund-manager holdings. Count each exposure once. Measure the gap against the relevant limit in percentage points, stating whether the denominator includes cash. With partial look-through, show the known minimum or range. Explain which risk an alternative diversifies and which risks remain. [FCA diversification guide](https://www.fca.org.uk/investsmart/diversification)
- **Fees.** Match share classes, index, currency hedging and distribution policy. Estimate annual fee savings as the amount affected times the difference in annual fee rates. Divide one-off switching costs by positive annual savings for a simple break-even period. Compare it with the intended holding period. Include spread, FX, platform fees or tax where evidenced. Fund returns after fees already include ongoing fund charges. [Vanguard on ETF costs](https://www.vanguard.co.uk/professional/vanguard-365/investment-knowledge/etf-knowledge/what-are-the-costs-of-ETFs)
- **Cash or yield.** Identify what cash is reserved, needed soon or available to move. Compare dated rates on eligible balances in the same currency, using the same annual-rate convention. State the difference in pounds over the relevant period, the access terms, how the money is held and how protection differs. Treat a variable rate as a scenario. For dividend ideas, compare total return including price changes and check whether cash flows support the payout. [Trading 212 cash custody and protection](https://helpcentre.trading212.com/hc/en-us/articles/40012724261661-Where-is-my-cash-held-and-how-is-it-protected)
- **Watchlist.** Include a named investment only with a reason it fits this portfolio, dated primary evidence, a measurable condition worth watching, and evidence that would defeat the case. Give the review date or next published report. A dated issuer filing or fund factsheet can support a research question. Recent price gains alone cannot establish an overlooked opportunity. When reliable evidence is unavailable, identify the missing fact. [SEC public filings](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings)

## Compare an earlier decision fairly

For “what did I miss?”, identify the alternative that was available and relevant **at the original decision date**. Show a retrospective comparison separately from today's forward-looking research. Match the dates, investable exposure, risk, cash interest or dividend reinvestment, fees, tax assumptions and reporting currency. Use an index in the reporting currency, or convert each valuation and dated flow using the rate on that date. For an unhedged foreign index, multiply its level by reporting-currency units per foreign unit. Use a hedged series for a hedged comparison. Keep currencies separate when rates are missing. Today's holdings alone do not establish past holdings or returns. [CFA Institute on benchmark selection and cash flows](https://www.gipsstandards.org/standards/gips-standards-for-firms/gips-standards-handbook-for-firms/) · [Bank of England historical exchange rates](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/statistics/exchange-rates)

To compare **money outcomes**, replay the same starting capital and each dated external deposit or withdrawal into the chosen total-return benchmark. Treat deposits as positive and withdrawals as negative. State the price/timing convention. Compare ending values in money. For returns, compare like-for-like time-weighted returns, or calculate money-weighted returns for both using the same dated flows. If the benchmark series or flow dates are missing, say what would be needed for a number. Avoid building an apparent track record from today's winners.

Use only the rows the evidence supports. Explain unfamiliar terms in the row where they first appear. For an exploratory question, keep the output to research and comparisons. Order approval belongs to a separate, explicit request.
